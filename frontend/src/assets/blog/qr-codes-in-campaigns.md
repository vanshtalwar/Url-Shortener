QR codes connect the physical world to a web page in one scan. Posters, packaging, menus, business cards, event badges and slides all become clickable. Used well, they're one of the most direct ways to turn real-world attention into a visit you can measure. Used badly, they're a square nobody scans.

## Why put a short link behind every QR code

A QR code simply encodes some text — usually a URL. You *could* encode your full destination address directly. Pointing the code at a short link instead has three big advantages:

1. **You can change the destination later.** Printed a thousand flyers and the landing page moved? Update where the short link points; the printed code keeps working.
2. **You can measure scans.** Every scan opens the short link, so it's counted like any other click — with time, device and approximate location.
3. **The code is simpler.** A short URL needs less data, which makes the code less dense and easier for cameras to read, especially when printed small.

## Where QR codes work well

QR codes shine when someone has a reason to act **right now** and a phone in their hand:

- **Packaging and inserts:** setup guides, warranty registration, reorder pages.
- **Events:** schedules, slides, feedback forms, Wi-Fi details.
- **Restaurants and retail:** menus, reviews, loyalty sign-ups.
- **Print ads and posters:** an offer page that's too long to type.
- **Presentations:** a link to the slides or a resource, shown on the final slide.

They work less well where people can't easily point a camera — on a fast-moving vehicle, high on a billboard, or on a screen that's already on their phone (just give them the link there).

## Making codes that scan first time

Most scanning problems come from how the code is printed, not the code itself.

### Size and distance

A useful rule of thumb: the code should be about **one tenth of the scanning distance** across. A code read from 1 metre away should be roughly 10 cm wide. On business cards and packaging, don't go below about 2 cm.

### Contrast

Dark code on a light background, always. Avoid low-contrast brand colours, and avoid inverting the code (light on dark) — some scanners still struggle with it.

### The quiet zone

Leave a clear margin around the code — at least four of its small squares wide. Text, borders or images crowding the edge are a common cause of failed scans.

### Test before you print

Print a sample at actual size and scan it with a couple of different phones, in the real lighting where it will be used. Check that it opens the right page and that the page loads quickly on mobile data.

## Tell people why to scan

A code on its own is a puzzle. A short line of text next to it turns it into a call to action:

- "Scan for today's menu"
- "Scan to register your warranty"
- "Scan for 10% off your next order"

Say what they'll get, keep it short, and put it right beside the code.

## Measuring QR campaigns

Because every scan goes through your short link, you can track a QR campaign the same way you track any link:

- **Use one short link per placement.** One for the poster at the entrance, one for the table cards, one for the packaging insert. Then each link's numbers tell you exactly which placement works.
- **Watch the time of day.** Scans that cluster around lunchtime or event start times tell you when people engage.
- **Check the device split.** QR traffic should be almost entirely mobile. If the landing page isn't fast and easy on a phone, that's where you'll lose people.
- **Compare unique visitors with total scans.** Many repeat scans from one visitor might mean the page didn't give them what they expected.

If the destination is your own website, you can also add UTM parameters to it — for example `utm_medium=print` and `utm_source=table_card` — so your site's analytics credit the visit correctly.

## Keeping codes useful over time

- **Don't let printed codes go dead.** If a promotion ends, point the short link at a sensible replacement page rather than letting it fail.
- **Use expiry deliberately.** For a time-limited offer, an expiry date stops people reaching an old deal — but make sure that's what you want for anything already printed.
- **Keep a list** of which code is printed where, so you know which link to update when things change.

## The takeaway

Put a short link behind every QR code, print it large and clear with a reason to scan, give each placement its own link, and watch the results like any other campaign. That turns a black-and-white square into one of the most measurable things you can print.
